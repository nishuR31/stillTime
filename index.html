The part most apps skip

Writing a setting isn't the same as the phone obeying it.

“I set it to 10 minutes” and “my phone now sleeps after 10 minutes” are two different claims. On plenty of devices a battery manager or a manufacturer policy quietly discards the change — and putInt still returns true.

So Stilltime keeps three numbers and never pretends they are the same.

  • 1

    Requested

    What you turned the dial to. Never shown as if it already happened.

  • 2

    Applied

    What Stilltime wrote and read back. If the read-back disagrees, it reports failure instead of claiming success.

  • 3

    Observed

    What the device holds right now, read live. This is the one that is allowed to call itself the truth.

How it works

Three steps, one system screen.

No root, no setup, no account. The only friction is a permission Android requires for every app that wants to change a system setting.

  1. STEP 01

    Grant the permission

    Android exposes “Modify system settings” as a revocable grant, not a popup. Tap once and a system screen naming Stilltime opens — nothing is ever granted silently, and you can take it back the same way.

  2. STEP 02

    Turn the dial

    The full circle covers the whole range, from 15 seconds to 1 hour. The scale is logarithmic, so a short timeout gets as much of the dial as a long one — no more crawling through a dead zone of minutes. Or just tap a preset.

  3. STEP 03

    Apply, then verify

    Stilltime writes the value and immediately reads it back. If a battery manager threw the change away, you find out here — with the live Battery Saver, Doze and OEM signals that explain why.

When it earns its place

The timeout you want depends on what you're doing.

Long builds and downloads

Wipe a device, flash firmware, leave a render going. Set 15 or 30 minutes and stop re-waking the screen.

Reading, recipes, calls

Start Stay Awake and the screen holds for 30 minutes, then puts your original value back by itself.

Presenting or mirroring

Demos, screen sharing, casting to a TV. The last thing you want mid-talk is a black screen.

Someone else's phone

Setting it for a parent or a child used to mean walking them through six levels of Settings. Now it's a dial.

Saving battery

15 seconds instead of 10 minutes adds up. Nothing on the device wakes up unless you touch it.

From the home screen

The widget renders with RemoteViews, so quick presets and Stay Awake keep working when the app itself is closed.

Features

Small app. Nothing it doesn't need.

No background service, no wake lock, no analytics, no network code in the release binary at all.

Logarithmic dial

Equal distance means equal ratio, so 15s→30s gets the same arc as 15m→30m. Fully operable by screen reader and arrow keys.

Stay Awake

A 30-minute session that remembers your old value and restores it. If you change the timeout elsewhere mid-session, it refuses to overwrite your choice.

Home-screen widget

Quick presets and a Stay Awake toggle, drawn without the React Native runtime so it survives the app being killed.

Power diagnostics

Live Battery Saver, Doze, battery-optimisation and OEM state, plus an explanation of which one is most likely deferring your write.

Screen-off test

Hidden behind seven taps on the build version. It listens for the system broadcast that fires when the screen actually goes dark and compares the real elapsed time against what you asked for.

In-app transparency

How-to-use, permissions, security notes, privacy and terms pages ship inside the app — readable without leaving it.

For the sceptical

Prove the phone actually listens.

A read-back proves the value was accepted. It does not prove the device will act on it. Stilltime can close that gap: Android broadcasts ACTION_SCREEN_OFF to any app listening for it, so the app can time the real thing.

Run the quick test, leave the phone alone, and come back. It tells you the exact duration before dark, the difference from what you asked for, and — if it ran short — that something else changed the setting back.

It refuses to print a number when the run was invalidated, because Android restarts its idle timer on any touch. An honest blank beats a confident wrong number.

Transparency

What it asks for, and what it refuses.

It asks for two things

  • WRITE_SETTINGS

    The only one it truly needs. Android requires it before any app may write SCREEN_OFF_TIMEOUT. Used for that one key and nothing else.

  • VIBRATE

    The small tap when you cross a dial detent or finish a write. Android refuses to vibrate without it.

And what it never asks for

  • ✕ INTERNET
  • ✕ ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE
  • ✕ SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW
  • ✕ EXTERNAL_STORAGE
  • ✕ CAMERA · MIC
  • ✕ LOCATION

A release build declares no INTERNET permission at all, so it is structurally incapable of making a network request — not merely choosing not to. You can check this yourself in the merged manifest.

Storage

One private SharedPreferences file in the app sandbox. Uninstall and it's gone.

Background

No service, no wake lock, no alarms. Closed means closed.

Escalation

No root, no hidden APIs, no accessibility service, no device admin.

Telemetry

No analytics, no crash reports, no update pings. There is nowhere for it to send anything.
